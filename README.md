# Quipu Statistical Analysis

**Reverse-engineering Inca accounting forms through structural analysis.**

The Inca quipu (khipu) is not a written language — it is a recording system. More spreadsheet than manuscript. This project treats it as such: extracting blank form templates from hundreds of filled specimens, testing color-commodity hypotheses statistically, and mapping the structural grammar of an empire-wide bureaucratic technology.

## Key findings

### Cord colors are ecologically patterned
Pale blue appears in 41% of highland quipu vs 0% of 183 coastal quipu (p=3.4e-10). Knot types show no zone pattern — the numerical system is universal, the color system is local. Colors encode *what*; knots encode *how many*.

### Six structural form types
612 quipu cluster into six blank templates: tally strip, flat register, simple list, grouped account, hierarchical ledger, deep hierarchy. Form and content are independent — the Inca separated document design from information type.

### Imperial grammar, local sentences
No two quipu are identical across museums, but the E-E-E marker appears in 16 quipu across 11 museums. Identical quantity templates (tribute quotas) appear in far-flung collections. Standardized bureaucracy.

### Garcilaso tested
Garcilaso de la Vega (1609) claimed yellow=gold, white=silver, red=soldiers. Yellow and white are untestable (preservation bias). Red *is* enriched at frontier zones but also at Pachacamac (religious center) — better interpretation: red marks state/imperial presence, not soldiers specifically.

## Method

Negative-correlation analysis (systematic absence as evidence), cross-collection pattern matching, structural clustering. All statistical tests with Bonferroni correction. Honest about limitations: n=1 highland site, no ground-truth commodity labels yet.

## Data

Harvard Khipu Database (Urton & Brezine), Open Khipu Repository. Analyses in reports/.
